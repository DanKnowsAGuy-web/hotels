A dollar you stop spending is worth twelve-fifty in what your hotel is worth.

Hotels trade on net operating income. At the roughly 8% cap rate the market pays,13 that's the whole arithmetic: a dollar of energy you never buy drops straight to NOI, and NOI capitalizes into about twelve and a half dollars of property value.

Meanwhile about a third of the energy an average commercial building buys does nothing at all.1 Most hotels have never had anyone qualified find out where theirs goes.

You've been pitched savings before, by someone selling a single product, with a payback chart from a different building. Saying no was the right call. But the bills keep arriving, in every property, every month.

+See why those pitches fail

Most energy vendors carry one product, so every property gets the same answer. The case study they show you is a different building's prescription. And the people who know your equipment best, your HVAC and mechanical service companies, are paid for service hours, not for kilowatt-hours you never use. Nobody in that model is paid to measure your properties first, so the waste survives the upgrade.

That is the entire difference here: the measurement comes first, and the fix is chosen to match what it finds, from more than four dozen technologies.

Most of your bill is the compressor. It runs whether it's cooling or not.

On most hotel systems it burns full power even when it isn't cooling anything. You pay for that either way. We take the cost out, and on the bigger systems we pull some of the heat the compressor needs from the sun. The room still sits at 72. The bill doesn't.

A hotel never closes.

At 2 a.m. the lobby is empty, the restaurant is dark, and the lot is still. The building isn't. Guest-room HVAC holds every occupied room, the hot water stays hot for the early flights, the corridors and life-safety systems never switch off. It's the one building type that runs every hour of the year, and the one place waste compounds around the clock.12

8,760hours a year the building never gets to rest. An office powers down at night and on weekends; a hotel never does.

This is why we start with the meter, not the roof: in a building that never rests, the fix attached to the load that never stops is the one that pays the most.

Your worst property is hiding in the average.

Same flag, same brand standard, the same equipment package on the roof. And a spread between your best property and your worst that routinely runs into double digits per available room. The portfolio report shows one number. The properties bleeding inside it are invisible.

Nobody ranks them, because ranking them means pulling twelve months of interval data from every meter in the portfolio, and it is nobody's job to go get it. It is the first thing we do, before a single piece of equipment is discussed.
